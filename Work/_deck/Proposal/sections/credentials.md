# Credentials

**Every reference carries it, two to four pages, on a `Section divider` titled
*Credentials*.** Three of four place it before the scope; one pairs it with the
team under *Team & credentials*. The buyer reads it to answer one question:
have they done this before, here, in my sector.

## Two page shapes

**The credential list.** Title *Selected [sector] credentials*, continued as
*(1/2)*, *(2/2)* where there are many. A one-line lead at y 2.70: *"We have
significant experience assisting clients in the [sector] sectors. The following
pages set out our selected credentials…"*. Beneath, a grid of credential cards —
client type, engagement, year — hand-built on `Title only_Blank`. Client names
appear only where the engagement is public or the client has consented; the
default is a descriptor: *Global optics and photographic equipment
manufacturer*.

**The case study.** Title *Case study – [engagement type]*. A header strip at
y 3.00 in three cells: *Sector*, *Project*, *Client* (as a descriptor). A
situation paragraph at y 4.70, 20.10 cm wide, three or four sentences. A *Why
KPMG?* box on the right. Then three columns at y 9.80, 6.40 cm each, under the
headers **Client challenges · Response · Outcomes**, three or four bullets in
each. Two case studies is the norm.

## Where the section sits in a strategy pursuit

One reference titles the pages *[Sector] deal & strategy experience (1/2)*
and puts them ahead of two case studies. Same shapes, different framing.

## Conventions

- One sector or service line per page. Mixed pages read as padding
- Every credential is dated or bracketed to a period
- The *Why KPMG?* box carries claims the case study then evidences. Three
  claims, each a sentence
- No client is named without consent. The descriptor is the default and reads
  as more discreet, which is itself a credential

## Checks

- Every credential connects to the engagement being pitched: same sector, same
  deal type, or same geography. Say which in the card
- The case studies are the two closest matches, and the *Outcomes* column
  carries a result the client can recognise as the one they want
