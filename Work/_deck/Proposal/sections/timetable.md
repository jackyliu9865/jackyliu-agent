# Timetable

**One page, in every reference**, titled *Envisaged timetable*, *Envisaged due
diligence timeline* or *Indicative timetable for key workstreams*. It sits
either as the first page of the fee section — the divider reads *Timetable and
fee estimate* or *Timetable and Commercials* — or as the first page of scope
where the pursuit is an integration.

## The page

A Gantt across the content width, hand-built on `Title only_Blank` or
`Two Columns Text`.

| Element | Geometry |
|---|---|
| Lead sentence | y 3.70, 14.10 to 28.30 cm wide — the caveat, see below |
| Week headers | y 8.70, one cell per week at 4.70 cm (six weeks) or 6.30 cm (four weeks), the last widened to *Week 6+* |
| Workstream bars | One row per workstream, left label, bar spanning its weeks |
| Milestones | A flag or a label — *Final report* at y 4.80 — on the week it lands |

## The caveat, which every reference carries

The lead sentence conditions the timetable on the client. The two forms seen:

- *"We are ready to adjust the timeline as needed by you and the process."*
- *"The timeline is based on our understanding of your internal timetable and
  is dependent on the timely provision of data by Target Management. We will
  not commence our work until we receive 80% of the information requested."*

The second is the stronger and belongs on any deal pursuit. The 80% threshold is
a house position and stays as written.

## Conventions

- Weeks, never dates. A proposal is issued before the start is agreed
- Workstream rows in the same order as the scope section and the fee table
- One milestone per deliverable named in the scope: draft report, final
  report, board paper

## Checks

- Every workstream in [[scope]] has a bar
- The final milestone is the deliverable the client asked for, in the week
  their own timetable needs it
- The caveat is present. A timetable with no condition on information becomes a
  commitment
