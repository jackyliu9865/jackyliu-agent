# Our approach

Two of four references carry it, two do not. It earns its place
when the method is a selling point — an integration roadmap, a proprietary
diligence framework — and is dropped when the scope pages already say how the
work runs.

Opens on a `Section divider` titled *Our approach*. Two to six pages.

## Two page shapes

**The roadmap.** A phased timeline across the full 28.40 cm width, hand-built on
`Title only_Blank`. Phase headers in a row at y 3.80, numbered activity cells
beneath in columns (*0.1 Confirm strategic rationale…*, *1.1 Launch execution
program…*), a *KEY OUTPUTS* row at the foot at y 12.80. Row labels at 1.10 cm on
the far left: *PHASE*, *KEY ACTIVITIES*, *KEY OUTPUTS*. A one-line lead at y 2.70
states what the roadmap shows. A footnote defines any term of art such as
*Day 1*.

**The four-box.** Where the approach is a set of workstreams rather than a
sequence: four boxes in a two-by-two at roughly 9.00 cm each, one per stream,
each a bold name and two sentences. A strapline at y 2.70 states the
integration claim — *"Seamless cooperation and operational synergies with KPMG
advising you on various streams."* A closing sentence at y 15.00 points forward
to the scope pages.

## Other pages seen

| Title | Content |

## Conventions

- Every page has a one-line lead at y 2.70 saying what the page shows
- KPMG assets are named as proper nouns and capitalised consistently
- Activity cells are numbered *phase.step* so the scope pages can reference them

## Checks

- The approach answers the needs stated in [[understanding]], in the same order
- Nothing here duplicates a scope page. The approach says *how we think*; scope
  says *what we will do*
- Where the section is dropped, the summary page has no *Our approach* column
