# Understanding

**The first section after the summary in every reference.** It proves the team
listened. Titled *Background*, *Overview* or *Overview of the transaction*, it
opens on a `Section divider` and runs one to three pages. One reference extends
it with a twelve-page outside-in market view; that is a strategy pursuit, and a
deal pursuit does not do it.

## The page

The lead page is titled **"Our understanding of…"** — *the transaction*, *the
proposed integration*, *your requirements*. Two shapes are used.

**Two columns, prose.** `One Column Text` or hand-built at 13.70 cm each. Left
column *Target background* or *Situation*; right column *Transaction overview
and rationale* or *Your objectives*. Three to five sentences each. A one-line
lead at y 2.70 dates the understanding: *"Based on our discussion on [date], we
have outlined below our understanding of…"*.

**A diagram.** Where the engagement is structural — an integration, a carve-out
— the page is a current-state / future-state picture on `Title only_Blank`:
entity boxes at 4.60 cm, row labels at 1.30 cm (*Current state*, *Future
state*, *Main activities*), a strategic-objectives column at 13.70 on the right.
Employee counts and entity names come from the supplied content and are
footnoted to their source.

## What follows the lead page

| Page | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| *What you need to get right* / *Prioritising your immediate needs* | Integration and transformation pursuits | Three to five boxes, each a need and a one-line KPMG response |
| *Key value drivers in the Target's industry* | Deal pursuits | A driver list on `One Column Text`, each with a sentence |
| *Our initial point of view* | Strategy pursuits, closing the outside-in section | Prose, `One Column Text` |
| *Why KPMG* | One reference, folded in here | Four to six reasons, hand-built boxes |

## Conventions

- The client is named once in full with its short form in quotes — *[Client
  Legal Name] Co., Ltd. ("[Short form]")* — and the short form thereafter.
  *"You"* in quotes is the collective client throughout the deck
- The target of a deal is *"Target"* or *"Target Group"* in quotes, defined on
  first use, never named where the deal is unannounced
- Every figure carries a footnote to its source at y 16.30: a website, a fact
  book, a discussion date

## Checks

- The understanding is dated or attributed to a conversation
- Nothing here is research. It restates what the client said and what the
  pursuit team was told
- The section ends on what the client needs, which is what the approach answers
